A big update on 3.18. Watch now shows what your music apps are playing, keeps one queue for videos, songs and podcasts, and works with your Mac's media keys. This covers everything since 3.18.

## What's new

### Music and podcasts

- **Now Playing.** Whatever Music, Spotify, Podcasts or another audio app is playing shows at the top of the panel with its artwork. Click it to open the artwork in a floating window that follows the music. Right-click for Copy, Save, Open in Preview, Search YouTube and Show App. Full screen (**F** or **⌃⌘F**) puts the artwork over a gradient of its own colours, or black. You can turn the row off in Preferences ▸ Show what's playing in other apps.
- **Controls in the music window.** Rest the pointer on the title and a row of controls slides in: previous, play/pause, next, a timeline you can drag, shuffle, repeat and volume. For Music and Spotify, shuffle, repeat and volume change that app's own settings, and macOS asks for permission the first time. A click anywhere plays or pauses, and a quick double-click opens the song in its app.
- **Podcasts.** Search results have **YouTube | Podcasts** tabs. Pick a show, or paste an Apple Podcasts link or a feed, and its episodes play in the music window and resume where you left off.
- **Your Music library.** A third search tab, **Music**, searches the songs in your library. Click one to play it in Music.

### One queue

- **Up Next** drops down under the music window (**⌥⌘U**). Add videos, audio files, podcast episodes and Spotify songs with **Play Next** and **Play Later** (right-click a row, or use the **+** button), with **⌥Return** in the search field, or by dropping files and links on the menu-bar icon. Drag rows to reorder them and press ⌫ to remove one. If the queue started while Music or Spotify was playing, that app carries on with its own queue when yours ends.
- Every video window has its own Up Next list too, also on **⌥⌘U**. Drop a video, file or link on it to play it next.
- The queue is kept if you close the music window or quit. Choose **Resume** next to "Up Next from before".

### Part of macOS

- Whatever Watch plays shows up in Now Playing and Control Center, and the media keys, AirPods and headphone buttons control it.
- Music or Spotify pauses when you start something in Watch and plays again when you close Watch's videos. Both can be switched off in Preferences ▸ General.
- Finder's **Open With ▸ Watch** plays a video in a window and sends audio files, folders and multiple files to Up Next.

### YouTube playback

- Links play in Watch's own player again and never fall back to YouTube's web page. When a video can't play, the window says why and offers **Sign In…**, **Try Again**, **Open in Browser** and **Copy Details**. If YouTube asks Watch to prove it isn't a bot and you've connected your account, Watch retries once with your sign-in.
- Quality follows the window. A small window plays a lower resolution, and making it bigger switches up to 1080p.
- Video windows have a volume slider: hover the speaker icon, or use ↑ and ↓. New windows start at your last volume.
- New windows open beside the others instead of on top of them.

## Install

1. Download **Watch-3.25.dmg** below (about 230 MB).
2. If Watch is running, quit it from its menu-bar icon.
3. Open the disk image and drag **Watch** onto **Applications**, choosing **Replace**. Your links, favourites and settings stay.
4. Open Watch. It isn't notarized, so if macOS blocks it, go to **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**.

## Known issues

- The Now Playing row depends on a workaround for the limit macOS 15.4 put on reading what other apps are playing. If a later macOS closes it, the row will stop showing up.
- YouTube sometimes asks to confirm you're not a bot, and it can block videos from certain networks. Signing in under Preferences ▸ YouTube Account helps.
- Automated tests run on macOS 15 on Apple Silicon. Real Intel Macs and macOS 13–14 haven't been tried.
- The music window's hover controls and click timing, and the media keys, haven't been tried by hand yet. The automated tests cover them with stand-ins for Music and Spotify.
